Toyota Hilux Diesel Tuning Brisbane — What a Remap Actually Does to a GUN125 or GUN126

By LP Tuning  •  6 min read  •  Southeast Queensland, Australia

The Toyota Hilux is Australia's best-selling ute for a reason. It's capable, reliable, and the 2.8-litre turbo diesel in the GUN125 and GUN126 is a genuinely strong engine. But Toyota leaves a significant amount of performance untouched at the factory, partly because they're engineering one tune to suit every market worldwide, and partly because there's a commercial logic to keeping the base model below the SR5 or GR Sport on the spec sheet.

An ECU remap changes that. Rather than buying a more expensive variant for the power, you unlock what's already there in your existing engine — at a fraction of the cost.

What Does a Hilux Remap Actually Do?

The ECU (Engine Control Unit) manages everything: fuel injection timing, injection pressure, boost pressure from the turbo, torque limiting, and the rev limiter. Toyota's factory calibration is conservative — deliberately so. An ECU remap replaces those parameters with a custom calibration built around the actual capability of the engine.

For the 2.8-litre diesel in the GUN125/GUN126, a typical remap delivers:

+25 kW Typical power gain
+80 Nm Typical torque gain
8–12% Fuel savings on highway

The torque gain matters most for everyday driving. The extra Nm comes in low in the rev range — exactly where you need it when you're pulling a trailer up a hill, loaded with gear, or doing slower-speed work. The factory tune holds the torque back in that range; the remap releases it where it's actually useful.

Is It Safe for the Engine?

This is the right question to ask, and the honest answer is: yes, when done properly. LP Tuning's calibrations stay within the design tolerances of the engine. We're not pushing the 2.8L past what the internals can handle — we're removing the factory's conservative headroom that exists for warranty and emissions reasons, not for mechanical ones.

The fuel injectors, turbocharger, and engine components in these Hilux models are built to handle the extra demand. A well-calibrated remap doesn't stress the engine — in some cases it reduces stress, because the engine is working more efficiently and spending less time labouring at partial throttle in the wrong gear.

Every LP Tuning remap comes with a 30-day money-back trial. Drive it in real conditions — towing, loaded, highway runs — and if you're not satisfied, we reverse it completely at no cost.

The Towing Difference

If you're using your Hilux to tow — a boat, a horse float, a camper trailer, a car trailer — the remap makes a noticeable real-world difference. The extra low-end torque means the engine doesn't have to work as hard when you're loaded. You spend less time in lower gears, the transmission shifts more smoothly, and you're not holding up traffic on hills.

Fuel consumption when towing also tends to improve, because the engine is operating more efficiently rather than thrashing in a lower gear to maintain speed. Several LP Tuning customers who do regular interstate towing with Hiluxes have reported 10–15% better fuel economy on towing runs after their remap.

What About the Automatic Transmission?

The 6-speed automatic in the GUN126 Hilux is well-matched to a remapped engine. The transmission itself isn't modified — only the engine ECU. The increased torque figure stays within what the transmission is designed to handle, so you're not creating a weak point. Many automatic Hilux owners find the gearbox behaviour actually improves after a remap, because the engine's increased output means the transmission doesn't have to hold lower gears as long under load.

A common question: is it better to remap, or to just buy a more powerful ute? The maths are straightforward. The difference in purchase price between a base Hilux and a top-spec GR Sport is $20,000–$30,000. A remap is $1,450.
