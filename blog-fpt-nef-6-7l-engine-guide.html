FPT NEF 6.7L Engine Guide: Case IH Puma and New Holland T7 Explained

By LP Tuning  •  6 min read  •  Queensland, Australia

The FPT NEF 6.7 litre is the engine behind a large share of Case IH and New Holland mid-range tractors. Case IH first used it in Maxxum tractors in 2003 and Puma tractors in 2007, and it has powered New Holland T7 tractors for years. This guide covers the basics, the issues we see and what a remap does on it.

NEF 6.7L at a Glance

ManufacturerFPT Industrial (part of the same group as Case IH and New Holland)
LayoutInline six cylinder, turbocharged and aftercooled
Displacement6.7 litres (a four cylinder 4.5 litre version also exists)
Fuel systemElectronic high-pressure common rail
Typical machinesCase IH Maxxum and Puma, earlier mid-size Magnum, New Holland T6 and T7
Emissions hardwareMany Tier 4 versions use FPT's SCR system with AdBlue rather than EGR. Newer Stage V versions add a particulate filter.

Figures are general and vary by version, rating and year. Check your engine plate for the exact model.

Which Machines Use It?

Look for the NEF 6.7 in Case IH Puma and six cylinder Maxxum tractors, earlier mid-size Magnum models and New Holland T6 and T7 tractors. Like most modern farm engines, it is built in several horsepower ratings, which leaves useful headroom for a tailored remap.

Common Issues We See

These are the problems we most often see on working machines with this engine. None of them are unique to it, but knowing them helps you plan maintenance and spot trouble early.

  • AdBlue (SCR) faults. Because many versions rely on SCR, NOx sensors, dosing modules and crystallised injectors are the most common cause of warnings and derates.
  • Poor quality AdBlue. Contaminated or old fluid can trigger quality faults and damage the dosing system.
  • DPF loading on Stage V versions. Light work and long idling stop the filter from burning clean.
  • Fuel contamination. Water and dirt in fuel are hard on common rail injectors and pumps. Keep filters fresh.

What a Remap Does on the NEF 6.7

A tailored remap adds torque low and in the middle of the rev range, so the tractor pulls harder without dropping revs and uses less fuel for the same work. Our New Holland T7 went from 125 to 160 HP (indicative) with an 8% fuel saving.

Common Questions

Which tractors use the FPT NEF 6.7 engine?

Case IH Puma and six cylinder Maxxum tractors, earlier mid-size Magnum models and New Holland T6 and T7 tractors are common examples. Check your engine plate to confirm.

Does the NEF 6.7 have EGR?

Many Tier 4 versions use FPT's SCR system with AdBlue instead of EGR. Newer Stage V versions also add a particulate filter.

Can the NEF 6.7 be remapped?

Yes. Our New Holland T7 went from 125 to 160 HP (indicative) with an 8% fuel saving.

Why does my Puma or T7 keep showing AdBlue warnings?

The usual causes are a failed NOx sensor, a crystallised dosing injector, a dosing module fault or poor quality AdBlue.

How much fuel can a remap save on an NEF 6.7 tractor?

On our own jobs we have measured 6% to 11%, with the best machines reaching up to 18%. Results vary by make, model and condition.
