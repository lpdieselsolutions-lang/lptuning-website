John Deere PowerTech 6.8L Engine Guide: Specs, Machines and Tuning

By LP Tuning  •  6 min read  •  Queensland, Australia

The John Deere PowerTech 6.8 litre, known by its model code 6068, is one of the most common engines on Australian farms. It powers many 6R and 6M series tractors and a long list of older Deere machines and industrial equipment. This guide covers what the engine is, where you will find it, the issues we see in the field and what an ECU remap does on it.

PowerTech 6.8L at a Glance

Engine code6068 (PowerTech 6.8L)
LayoutInline six cylinder, turbocharged and aftercooled
Displacement6.8 litres
Fuel systemHigh-pressure common rail on modern versions
VersionsPowerTech E and Plus (older), PVS (variable geometry turbo) and PSS (series turbochargers)
PowerFrom about 140 hp up to about 300 hp in industrial ratings, depending on version and rating
Emissions hardwareFinal Tier 4 and Stage V versions use cooled EGR, a DOC, a DPF and SCR with AdBlue (DEF)

Figures are general and vary by version, rating and year. Check your engine plate for the exact model.

Which Machines Use It?

You will find the 6.8L in many mid and upper John Deere 6R and 6M tractors, earlier 6030 and 7030 series tractors, and a range of Deere industrial and construction equipment. The same engine is built in several horsepower ratings, and much of the difference between ratings is in the software. That is why the 6.8L responds so well to a tailored remap.

Common Issues We See

These are the problems we most often see on working machines with this engine. None of them are unique to it, but knowing them helps you plan maintenance and spot trouble early.

  • DPF loading on light work. Long idle periods, loader work and light PTO jobs keep exhaust temperatures low, so the DPF struggles to regenerate.
  • EGR valve and cooler soot. Soot build up can cause rough running, power loss and EGR fault codes.
  • AdBlue system faults. NOx sensors, dosing units and crystallised injectors are the usual suspects when an AdBlue warning appears.
  • Injector wear. Poor fuel quality and water in fuel shorten injector life. Good filtration matters.

What a Remap Does on the 6.8L

A tailored remap adjusts fuelling, boost and timing within safe limits so the engine makes more torque lower in the rev range. On the farm that means the tractor holds a gear under load, lugs less and uses less fuel for the same work. Our John Deere 6115M went from 115 to 130 HP (indicative) with an 11% fuel saving.

Common Questions

What is the John Deere 6068 engine?

The 6068 is John Deere's PowerTech 6.8 litre inline six cylinder diesel. It powers many 6R and 6M tractors and a wide range of Deere industrial equipment.

What is the difference between PowerTech PVS and PSS?

PVS uses a single variable geometry turbocharger. PSS uses two turbochargers in series for higher power and better response. Both are 6.8 litre engines.

Does the 6.8L have a DPF and AdBlue?

Final Tier 4 and Stage V versions use cooled EGR, a DOC, a DPF and SCR with AdBlue. Older versions have less after-treatment.

Can the PowerTech 6.8L be remapped?

Yes. It responds very well to a tailored remap because the same engine is built in several ratings. Our John Deere 6115M went from 115 to 130 HP (indicative) with an 11% fuel saving.
